Reading responses

Reading 1: Programming Design Systems

"1. There is a need for educational material that teaches the fundamentals of graphic design in a modern way, especially if it also gives students mental models for critiquing digital design projects."

Response: I couldn’t agree more with this quote. As the field of graphic design expands to the digital space, it is important to adapt the curriculum to keep up with the industry expectations.

"2. We are now in transition from an object-oriented to a systems-oriented culture. Here change emanates, not from things, but from the way things are done."

Response: I like how the author summarizes the transition in the field of graphic design to think and design systematically as digital products are on the rise. I’d like to learn more about how design systems can benefit the field of graphic and visual design.

"3. After all, this is easier than adopting a whole new set of processes. However, the fundamental problem with this approach is that static design tools like Illustrator and Sketch fail at prototyping digital systems."

Response: I really like this quote. By using the limitations of Illustrator and Sketch for prototyping systems, the author gave us a metaphor that implies the education system and the need to adapt to digital systems.

Reading 2: What Do Prototypes Prototype

"1. Every aspect of the system must be designed (or inherited from a previous system), and many features need to be evaluated in combination with others."

Response: I find this quote a great reminder for designing products that attempts to create an all-in-one workplace. It is important to consider the cognitive load of unwanted features and what the user truly wants.

"2. It was more efficient to wait on the results of independent investigations in the key areas of role, look and feel and implementation than to try to build a monolithic prototype that integrated all features from the start."

Response: It is a great tip for designers who work in agile, fast-paced environments. The learning and failing process from user testing can be more efficient than trying to build everything at once with personal biases.

"3. This is an example of a look and feel prototype. The virtue of the prototype was that it enabled a novel user interface design to be developed without having first to implement complex underlying technologies. While the role was inherited from existing fashion design practice, the prototype also demonstrated new options offered by the new computer-based approach."

Response: I really like the concept of a “look and feel prototype”. When exploring a creative field, it's important to discover possibilities in approaches rather than focus on the technology itself, which might restrict the creative process.
